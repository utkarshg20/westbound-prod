# Addendum B to SAMMY_STUDIO_SERVER_SPEC_v1 (30 Sep 2026): three graded locks, and five things the engine must own

From the Sammy Rane studio (Claude Code on Dan's Mac), at Dan's word. Zero credits all week; every render from a gated cut sheet.

B1. What was made. Play His Part (Scoot Inn, 3:58) got its 9:16 and a grade, and the Whisky widescreen got the same grade. Dan locked three new files on 30 Sep: Play His Part 9:16 graded, Play His Part 16:9 graded, Whisky v16r graded; YouTube masters of both Play His Part versions. The grade = Super Scale 2x + one room LUT + a per-shot brightness match, all rendered by DaVinci Resolve from the sheet. The ungraded locks stand beside them.

B2. The reframe detector was wrong on every band shot. On Play His Part the largest frontal face in a guitarist-with-drummer-behind frame was the drummer, so the detector centred Rex (rule 29) in 8 slots and cut the lead player; in the three-man outro it centred the man in the middle. All band windows were set by eye on the featured player and recorded with the reason. New rule from the render read: the whole stage holds inside the window. A window that keeps the drummer out can still keep the edge of his kit, which reads as an empty kit; the fix was a tighter 9:16 box (320x568 from the top of a 1344x768 take), recorded as a box. A 16:9 crop that hid an empty kit (zoom 1.7) did not survive the centre crop to 9:16; it needed its own box. Spec 10.3: windows can be boxes, not only horizontal slides; a band window is never the detector's; the frame review (10.4) reads every shot from the RENDER in its own shape (first/middle/last), which is what found the kit edges after a full-height window had passed every number.

B3. Resolve's Tilt is not in timeline pixels. On a 1080x1920 timeline (source 1344x768 on a FIT base, 1080x617), Tilt -338 moved the picture down only ~110 px; the compare named exactly the 720 zoomed frames. Measured on two renders: one unit of Tilt moves the picture (base-scaled picture height / timeline height) output px (0.3214 there); the sign was right; Pan was exact at 80..802. Spec 6 (renderer adapter): the unit model and a fixture (a sheet with a vertical offset on a portrait timeline; expected: renderer_agreement PASS). The compare is what caught it; renderer_agreement stays mandatory.

B4. A gate showed a stale image. The cut gate sampled past a take's end (the slot holds the last frame), ffmpeg wrote nothing, and the gate read a PNG left in a shared /tmp folder by the previous cut's gate: a Scoot Inn crowd frame appeared on the Whisky's evidence sheet, and the 28 Sep Whisky sheet had the same stale tile. Fixed: the time is clamped to the take's last frame, every run has its own temp folder, a missing frame is a refusal. Spec 7 (evidence): evidence images are content-addressed (hash of source, time, window), never shared paths; a missing evidence image fails the gate. Fixture: a slot longer than its take.

B5. The grade is measured, per room, previewed. A full colour match dragged Dan's neutral Midjourney band frames toward the red cast of the Nano Banana ones and warmed Sammy's close-ups; brightness-only (one slope on all channels) keeps each shot's colour. The Whisky's own LUT pushed faces orange; the lighter Scoot Inn LUT was used for both, by Dan's "the same way". Preview before render on the brightest, darkest and most neutral shots. Spec 10.2: the grade block (match mode, LUT recipe) lives in the sheet; a preview is part of the request.

B6. Small facts for the adapters. A cut's duration is checked in whole frames (238.15 s = 5715.6 frames renders 5716) plus Resolve's AAC tail; a master that ends inside the last frame is placed for its whole frames. The compare's cut detector can see motion inside a zoomed slot as a cut in one render and not the other: renderer_agreement judges the sheet's cuts and reports extra detections as notes. A Resolve render (H.264 High, BT.709 tagged, ~11 Mbps, AAC 48 kHz, index first) already is a YouTube master; byte copy, no re-encode.

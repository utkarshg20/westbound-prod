# Addendum A to SAMMY_STUDIO_SERVER_SPEC_v1 (28 Sep 2026): what the first Resolve day proved, and what it changes in the spec

From the Sammy Rane studio (Claude Code on Dan's Mac), at Dan's word, 28 Sep 2026. Section numbers refer to SAMMY_STUDIO_SERVER_SPEC_v1_2026-09-27.pdf. Everything below was measured today on the Whisky (Radio Underwater live, 41 shots, 254 s), zero credits spent, the locked v16 untouched.

## A1. What was measured

1. **Resolve reproduces a cut sheet frame for frame.** cuts/whisky_v16r.json built in DaVinci Resolve Studio 21.1 through its MCP (project, media pool, timeline, per-clip transform, two Fairlight tracks, render queue) from a generated script, rendered, and compared with our ffmpeg render of the same sheet: 6096 frames both, all 40 cuts on the same frames, mean SSIM 0.98, audio offset 0.0 ms. Render time 24 s plain, about 125 s with Super Scale 2x. Verdict PASS on the first render.
2. **The grade pass is real and it is ours, not Resolve's.** build/shot_match.py measures every shot on its source pixels (mean luma, RGB balance, grouped by who is in frame from TAKES_REGISTRY) and writes a per-shot ASC CDL into the sheet; Resolve applies it on node 1 with one LUT after it, Super Scale 2x on every source, and a crowd/room bed built from real recordings (build/live_bed.py). 31 of 41 shots corrected; the dark close-ups now sit with the rest, the band wides come down to the room, the upscale adds real detail. Dan's first reaction to a LUT-only pass was "it did literally nothing"; the measured pass is the one that worked. Lesson for the spec: the renderer executes numbers; the numbers must come from measurement in the engine.
3. **Resolve's editorial features are not usable through the API.** TimelineItem.SmartReframe() returns True and every reframed clip renders shifted down with a black band across the top (FILL and USE_PROJECT scaling; with FIT it does nothing). Two test renders proved it. It is banned from the generator.
4. **Reframing had to be built here, and the detector alone was not enough.** build/reframe_916.py (OpenCV Haar in its own process; opencv-python-headless 5.0 dropped CascadeClassifier and its libav clashes with PyAV) places a 9:16 window per shot on the largest readable frontal face in the upper 60 percent of the frame, present in at least half of 8 samples; crowd-only shots keep the centre. That rule is right on one-face shots (29 of 33). It is wrong on every profile two-shot (both men face each other, the drummer on his riser is the only frontal face, so the window sat on Rex, rule 29 broken) and on the solo (the largest face was the guitarist, not the soloist). Those four were set by measuring gridded source frames: Cass head x~400, Rex ~600, Max ~880, Lizzy ~530 of 1344; a 432 px window cannot hold two men 470 px apart, so the two-shots alternate one man each. Every window is recorded in the sheet as an explicit zoom with its provenance (detector or by-eye plus the measurement), and cut.py and the Resolve generator place the same pixels from it.
5. **The errors were found by looking at rendered frames, not by numbers.** Three rounds of rule tuning passed every check; a 30-point read of the render against the centre crop found all four in ten minutes. And the first review sheet squashed 9:16 frames to 16:9 boxes and hid the horizontal error at a glance (build/before_after.py is now portrait-aware).
6. **Renderer facts that belong in the adapter, learned the expensive way (in time, not credits):** MediaPool.ImportMedia takes a plain list of paths (the dict form returns None in 21.1); AppendToTimeline endFrame is exclusive; SetCDL and SetLUT coexist on node 1 (CDL first); SetClipProperty("Super Scale", 2) with an int; generate_lut writes to LUT/MCP and needs RefreshLUTList; Resolve crops the input-sized picture to the canvas BEFORE the Edit transform, so a Pan cannot reach a region outside the fill window (the generator bases such a shot on FIT); render settings are all-or-nothing dicts. Also found on the way: cut.py's frame index truncated 24.999999999999996 to 24, a one-frame judder on 35 frames of the locked v16 that nobody saw at speed. One shared frame-maths function (cut.src_index) now feeds every renderer.

## A2. What changes in the spec

**Section 6, provider adapters.** Add a provider class `renderer` with one implementation, Resolve, next to the generation providers. Interface: build_timeline(cut_id) -> timeline id; render(cut_id, out_name) -> job id; status(job id); compare(cut_id, render_a, render_b) -> PASS/FAIL with the numbers in A1.1. Implementation = the generated-script route (build/resolve_sheet_to_scripts.py: sheet in, three scripts out, submitted verbatim to run_script; the script reports problems and placements, never improvises). The sheet's `resolve` block (lut, cdl, superscale, reframe, reframe_by_eye) is the whole of what the renderer is told. **No editorial feature of any renderer is ever called** (Smart Reframe, auto colour, auto cut). ffmpeg stays the reference renderer; a change of renderer requires a compare PASS on the same sheet.

**Section 10.2, render.** render(cut_id) gains a `renderer` field (ffmpeg | resolve). The manifest gains renderer version, Resolve project, timeline and job ids, and the compare result when both renderers have rendered the sheet. Derivative names (`<cut>_<suffix>`) ride the parent's gate pass for show and send only, never for a write (already enforced by the hook today).

**New section 10.3, the reframe service (the engine's own module, not a renderer feature).** Input: a cut sheet and a target frame. Per shot: (a) one-face shots: the detector rule in A1.4, with the sample count, size threshold and vertical band as config; (b) two-shots and solos: never the detector; the subject is chosen from a gridded source contact sheet (one row per take, 100 px grid) and recorded with the measurement; one subject per window; consecutive two-shots alternate subjects; the drummer is never a subject (rule 29 as code); (c) crowd-only shots keep the centre; (d) shots that already carry a crop that hides someone are never touched. Output: an explicit zoom per shot written into the sheet with provenance, so every renderer places the same pixels and the window can be audited later. Portrait-aware evidence sheets.

**New section 10.4, the frame review gate.** Before any render reaches Dan: rendered frames read against the source (or against the previous version) at every window and every cut boundary, on a sheet that keeps the frame's shape; a reviewer (a person, or a model named as reviewer) writes what they saw; the read row is required in addition to the cut gate. This is the evidence rule of section 7 applied to framing, and it is the gate that would have caught all four errors of A1.4 before a render was shown.

**Section 7, gates.** Add frame_review (10.4) and renderer_agreement (the compare of A1.1, required whenever a sheet is rendered by a renderer other than the reference).

**Section 1.1, components that already work.** Add: resolve_sheet_to_scripts.py, shot_match.py, live_bed.py, reframe_916.py + _faces.py, before_after.py, compare_renders.py. All in build/, all read-only on exports, all gated by the hook. The engine absorbs them; it does not reinvent them.

**Section 13.3, replayed failures.** Add six fixtures dated 28 Sep: Smart Reframe black top (expected: renderer editorial call refused at validate); drummer as the only frontal face in a profile two-shot (expected: reframe refuses a window whose subject is Rex, asks for a grid read); a by-eye window guessed from a 16:9 contact sheet landing on the wrong person (expected: by-eye windows require a source-grid measurement in the record); a squashed portrait evidence sheet (expected: evidence sheets keep the frame's shape, test asserts aspect); input crop before transform giving a black third (expected: fill-or-fit base chosen from the region, test on the G3 slot); the float-truncation judder (expected: one frame-maths function, property test that src_index(start, i) is monotone and hits every source frame at 24 fps).

**Section 14, delivery plan.** Move "9:16 reframes with subject tracking" from Phase 2 into M3 (gates, review, render), because it exists today as scripts and the engine should absorb it in the same milestone as the render service. M3 acceptance adds: the Whisky v17 sheet rendered through the service by both renderers with compare PASS; the vertical's 33 windows reproduced from the sheet alone; a frame_review row present before the export can be shown. Add to the do-not-build list: do not depend on a renderer's editorial features.

**Appendix B, rules as code.** Rule 29 (Rex background only) gains a check in the reframe service; a new line for the two-shot rule (a portrait window holds one subject; two-shots alternate; the choice and the measurement are recorded); a new line for "windows are recorded, never implicit" (a renderer given a sheet without a window for a shot renders the centre and says so).

## A3. What a renderer cannot fix, for the record

The pre-lock bushy-beard Max in every two-shot of the locked Whisky, and the synchronised crowd and band bobbing (rule 22). Those are new takes, credits and Dan's word. The engine's job is to make that spend land right the first time; today's work made the render side of that deterministic and gated. The judgement side (who is the subject, is the frame right, is the face his) is the part the spec was written for, and today confirmed it is not something a rented tool will do for us.
